Section

Computer Networks

Layers, TCP/IP, routing, and the path a packet takes from socket to wire.

  1. 01What is Networking?Independent machines exchanging messages over a shared, unreliable medium.
  2. 02LANA local area network: one broadcast domain or a few, under one administration.
  3. 03WANA wide-area network spanning sites and usually several providers.
  4. 04MANA metropolitan network: a city-scale fabric, often provider-owned fiber.
  5. 05PANA personal area network: Bluetooth, USB, the devices around one person.
  6. 06Network TopologyBus, star, ring, mesh, hybrid — how nodes are wired, logically and physically.
  7. 07Client/ServerA role split: clients initiate, servers wait and respond. Most of the internet.
  8. 08Peer-to-PeerNodes that are both client and server. BitTorrent, some overlay networks, some blockchains.
  9. 09Network DevicesThe box-level view: hubs, switches, routers, firewalls, and where each sits in the stack.
  10. 10HubA physical-layer repeater: every bit out every port. Collision domain = the whole hub.
  11. 11SwitchA data-link device that forwards frames by MAC and splits collision domains.
  12. 12RouterA network-layer device that forwards packets between subnets using a routing table.
  13. 13BridgeConnects two L2 segments and learns MACs. A switch is a multi-port bridge.
  14. 14GatewayA node that translates between networks or protocols — often just 'the router'.
  15. 15RepeaterAmplifies or retimes a signal to extend a physical segment.
  16. 16ModemModulator-demodulator: analog carrier on, bits off, and the reverse.
  17. 17Access PointA bridge between wireless stations and a wired LAN, with association and encryption.
  18. 18FirewallA policy box: filter, NAT, or proxy traffic by rule. Host or network, stateful or not.
  19. 19OSI ModelSeven layers of function, from photons to application messages. The shared language of networking.
  20. 20Layer 7 — ApplicationThe OSI layer where applications meet the network: HTTP, DNS, SMTP, and their data.
  21. 21Layer 6 — PresentationSyntax and encryption: encoding, compression, TLS as it is often taught at this layer.
  22. 22Layer 5 — SessionDialogs: setup, teardown, and checkpoints. Thin in TCP/IP, explicit in some RPC systems.
  23. 23Layer 4 — TransportProcess-to-process: ports, reliability, and congestion control. TCP and UDP live here.
  24. 24Layer 3 — NetworkHost-to-host across hops: IP, routing, ICMP. Packets and logical addresses.
  25. 25Layer 2 — Data LinkHop-to-hop frames: MAC addresses, Ethernet, switches, and error detection.
  26. 26Layer 1 — PhysicalBits on a medium: voltage, light, radio, connectors, and the limits of Shannon.
  27. 27TCP/IP ModelFour layers the internet actually runs: application, transport, internet, network access.
  28. 28OSI vs TCP/IPA seven-layer teaching model versus a four-layer running model. Same ideas, different cuts.
  29. 29SignalsThe physical representation of bits: analog vs digital, encoding, and noise.
  30. 30BandwidthHow much spectrum or bit-rate a channel offers. Not the same thing as throughput.
  31. 31LatencyOne-way or round-trip delay. Propagation, transmission, queueing, processing.
  32. 32Transmission MediaCopper, fiber, and wireless — each with a noise, cost, and distance story.
  33. 33FiberLight in glass. Huge bandwidth, long reach, immunity to electrical noise.
  34. 34CopperTwisted pair and coax. Cheap, familiar, and still how most desks are wired.
  35. 35WirelessShared spectrum, fading, and a MAC that is nothing like a wire.
  36. 36ModulationImprinting bits on a carrier: AM, FM, PSK, QAM — the physical alphabet.
  37. 37FramesThe data-link PDU: header, payload, trailer. Ethernet's 14-byte header is the one you will draw.
  38. 38MAC Addresses48-bit link-layer identifiers. Unique-ish, local or universal, unicast or group.
  39. 39EthernetThe LAN technology: CSMA/CD history, switching present, 802.3 framing.
  40. 40SwitchingLearn MACs, flood unknowns, forward knowns, filter the rest. CAM tables and STP.
  41. 41ARPResolve IPv4 to MAC on a LAN. The cache, the timeout, and the spoof.
  42. 42Error DetectionParity, checksums, CRC. Detecting corruption is cheaper than preventing it.
  43. 43VLANsOne physical switch, many broadcast domains. 802.1Q tags and trunk ports.
  44. 44IPThe internet's network protocol: best-effort datagrams and hierarchical addresses.
  45. 45IPv432-bit addresses, dotted quad, fragmentation, and a header you should be able to sketch.
  46. 46IPv6128-bit addresses, no NAT-as-crutch, SLAAC, and a simpler header.
  47. 47SubnettingCarving a prefix into smaller networks. Masks, hosts, and the all-zero/all-one rules.
  48. 48CIDRClassless addressing: a prefix and a length. Aggregation is the point.
  49. 49RoutingChoosing a next hop. Tables, longest prefix match, and intra- vs inter-domain.
  50. 50ICMPIP's control plane for errors and diagnostics. Ping and traceroute ride it.
  51. 51NATRewriting addresses (and usually ports) so many hosts share a public IP.
  52. 52Routing ProtocolsRIP, OSPF, BGP: how routers learn paths they did not configure by hand.
  53. 53TCPReliable, ordered, congestion-controlled byte streams between two ports.
  54. 54UDPDatagrams with ports and a checksum. No connection, no retransmission, no congestion control.
  55. 55Ports16-bit transport addresses that multiplex a host. Well-known, registered, ephemeral.
  56. 56TCP HandshakeSYN, SYN-ACK, ACK. Sequence numbers exchanged so a connection can start.
  57. 57TCP TeardownFour-way (or three-way with FIN-ACK) close, TIME-WAIT, and why half-open exists.
  58. 58Flow ControlStopping a fast sender from drowning a slow receiver. TCP's window.
  59. 59Congestion ControlProtecting the network from collapse: slow start, AIMD, ECN, and BBR.
  60. 60DNSThe hierarchical lookup that turns names into records — usually addresses.
  61. 61DHCPAutomatic host configuration: address, mask, gateway, DNS, lease.
  62. 62HTTPThe application protocol of the web: methods, status codes, headers, and now HTTP/2 and /3.
  63. 63HTTPSHTTP over TLS. Confidentiality, integrity, and (usually) server authentication.
  64. 64FTPA historic file protocol with separate control and data channels. Prefer SFTP/HTTPS.
  65. 65SMTPHow mail moves between MTAs. Store-and-forward, MX records, and spam as an adversarial load.
  66. 66SSHAuthenticated, encrypted remote shells and tunnels. Keys, not passwords, if you can.
  67. 67WebSocketsA TCP-backed, full-duplex channel started with an HTTP upgrade.
  68. 68Wi-Fi802.11: association, airtime, and a MAC that is nothing like a switched Ethernet.
  69. 69BGPThe internet's inter-domain routing protocol: path vectors, policy, and the default-free zone.
  70. 70OSPFA link-state IGP: flood LSAs, run Dijkstra, install routes in the RIB.
  71. 71Spanning TreeA loop-free active topology on a switched LAN. Without it, broadcasts amplify until the LAN dies.
