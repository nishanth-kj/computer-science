Internal template for preparing a fact-based sponsorship pitch for the Computer Science Handbook. Complete this with current, exported analytics data before sending it to a prospective sponsor. Do not publish or send placeholders as if they were results.

## Possible collaboration types

- Sponsored tutorial/code walkthrough with clear sponsor disclosure.
- Relevant educational tool demo or interactive-lab collaboration.
- Clearly labelled sponsorship for a subject-specific learning resource.
- Original technical educational content commissioned by a partner, subject to editorial review.

Only offer a placement that has actually been implemented and can be delivered. Do not promise newsletter sponsorship before a newsletter is active. Do not promise specific impressions, clicks, rankings, or conversions without enough historical evidence and a written measurement plan.

## Editorial and disclosure policy

- Identify sponsorship near the relevant content, before the reader engages with the placement.
- Retain editorial control over correctness, limitations, comparisons, and technical conclusions.
- Reject products that are misleading, unsafe, unrelated to the readership, or make unsupported claims.
- Agree on the exact deliverable, publication date, campaign duration, destination URL, reporting, revision scope, cancellation terms, and payment terms in writing.
- Follow applicable disclosure, advertising, tax, and consumer-protection rules.

## Pricing worksheet

Do not set or present a rate card until there is credible audience data and a clear deliverable. Base a quote on:
- Production/review hours and expertise required.
- Relevant audience fit and verified reach.
- Placement duration and exclusivity, if any.
- Whether the deliverable is a one-off or recurring campaign.
- Clear success measures that are actually measurable.

For an early campaign without reliable traffic evidence, propose a limited pilot with a specific deliverable and transparent price; do not imply it buys guaranteed reach.

## Sponsor inquiry checklist

Ask the prospective sponsor for:
1. Organization and product.
2. Campaign goal and intended audience.
3. Proposed deliverable and target URL.
4. Timeline and desired run length.
5. Budget range.
6. Claims, assets, and approval requirements.
7. Tracking/measurement expectations and privacy constraints.
